Induction & Recursion

Mathematical Induction — The Method

To prove a statement $P(n)$ is true for all integers $n \ge n_0$:

  • Base case: show $P(n_0)$ is true (usually $n_0 = 0$ or $1$).
  • Inductive step: assume $P(k)$ is true (the induction hypothesis), then prove $P(k+1)$.
$\big[\,P(n_0)\ \wedge\ \forall k\,(P(k)\Rightarrow P(k+1))\,\big]\ \Rightarrow\ \forall n \ge n_0,\ P(n)$

Strong (complete) induction

Assume $P(n_0), P(n_0+1), \ldots, P(k)$ are all true to prove $P(k+1)$. Use it when $P(k+1)$ depends on several earlier cases (e.g. Fibonacci, prime factorization).

Counting

Fundamental Counting Principles

  • Product rule: if a task is a sequence of independent choices with $n_1, n_2, \ldots, n_k$ options, the total is $n_1 \cdot n_2 \cdots n_k$.
  • Sum rule: if a task can be done in one of several disjoint ways with $n_1, \ldots, n_k$ options, the total is $n_1 + n_2 + \cdots + n_k$.
Number of bit strings of length $n$ = $2^n$  •  Number of subsets of an $n$-set = $2^n$
"and / each step" → multiply  |  "or / one of" → add.

Pigeonhole Principle & Special Cases

If $n$ objects are placed into $k$ boxes, then at least one box contains at least $\left\lceil \dfrac{n}{k} \right\rceil$ objects.

To guarantee two items share a category with $k$ categories, you need $k+1$ items.
  • 13 people → two share a birth month (12 months).
  • Arrangements of a word with repeats: divide by the factorials of repeats, e.g. LEVEL = $\dfrac{5!}{2!\,2!} = 30$.
  • Diagonals of a convex $n$-gon: $\binom{n}{2} - n$.

Graphs

Graph Basics & Handshaking

  • The degree $\deg(v)$ is the number of edges at vertex $v$.
  • Handshaking lemma: $\displaystyle\sum_{v}\deg(v) = 2|E|$.
  • Therefore the number of odd-degree vertices is always even.
Complete graph $K_n$ has $\dbinom{n}{2}=\dfrac{n(n-1)}{2}$ edges — also the max edges of a simple graph on $n$ vertices.
From handshaking: a graph with $6$ vertices each of degree $3$ has $\dfrac{6\cdot 3}{2}=9$ edges.

Graphs

Trees, Euler & Hamilton

Trees

A tree is a connected acyclic graph. A tree with $n$ vertices has exactly $n-1$ edges.

Euler (edges)

  • Euler circuit (every edge once, returns to start) $\Leftrightarrow$ connected and every vertex has even degree.
  • Euler path $\Leftrightarrow$ connected and exactly 0 or 2 vertices have odd degree.

Hamilton (vertices)

A Hamiltonian path visits every vertex exactly once; a Hamiltonian circuit returns to the start. (No simple degree test — be careful not to confuse with Euler.)

Planarity & Coloring

Planar graphs

  • A graph is planar if it can be drawn with no crossing edges.
  • Euler's formula: for a connected planar graph, $v - e + f = 2$.
  • Consequence: $e \le 3v - 6$. So $K_5$ and $K_{3,3}$ are not planar (Kuratowski).

Coloring

  • The chromatic number $\chi(G)$ is the fewest colors so adjacent vertices differ.
  • A graph is bipartite $\Leftrightarrow$ it has no odd cycle $\Leftrightarrow$ it is 2-colorable. So a bipartite graph with an edge has $\chi = 2$.
