1. Sum rule — "OR" means add

$N = N_A + N_B$ for disjoint cases.
Exercise 1.1. A student buys one laptop: $7$ Windows models or $4$ Mac models are available. How many possible choices?
Correction

The two catalogues are disjoint and exactly one laptop is bought — "or" → add:

$7 + 4 = \boxed{11}$ choices.

Exercise 1.2. An elective must be picked from $5$ art classes, $6$ music classes or $3$ theater classes. How many options?
Correction

Three disjoint lists, one pick: $5 + 6 + 3 = \boxed{14}$.

Exercise 1.3. To go from Casablanca to Marrakech one can take one of $6$ trains, $8$ buses or $2$ flights. How many ways to make the trip?
Correction

One trip = one of the disjoint options: $6 + 8 + 2 = \boxed{16}$ ways.

2. Product rule — "THEN / AND" means multiply

$N = m \cdot n$ for sequential independent steps.
Exercise 2.1. An outfit is a shirt, a pair of pants and a pair of shoes. With $5$ shirts, $4$ pants and $3$ pairs of shoes, how many outfits?
Correction

Three successive independent choices — "and" → multiply:

$5 \times 4 \times 3 = \boxed{60}$ outfits.

Exercise 2.2. A restaurant offers $4$ starters, $6$ mains and $3$ desserts. How many complete meals (one of each)?
Correction

$4 \times 6 \times 3 = \boxed{72}$ meals.

Exercise 2.3. A license plate is $2$ letters followed by $3$ digits. How many plates are possible?
Correction

Each slot is independent: $26 \times 26 \times 10 \times 10 \times 10 = 26^2 \cdot 10^3 = \boxed{676{,}000}$.

3. Ordered + repetition → $n^k$

$k$ slots, $n$ symbols each, repeats allowed: $n^k$.
Exercise 3.1. How many $5$-digit PIN codes exist?
Correction

$5$ slots, $10$ digits each, repetition allowed: $10^5 = \boxed{100{,}000}$.

Exercise 3.2. A true/false test has $10$ questions. How many answer sheets are possible?
Correction

Each question: $2$ choices, $10$ questions: $2^{10} = \boxed{1{,}024}$.

Exercise 3.3. How many words of length $3$ over the alphabet $\{a, b, c, d\}$ (repeats allowed)?
Correction

$4^3 = \boxed{64}$ words.

4. Arrange all $n$ → $n!$

$n$ distinct objects in a row: $n!$ arrangements.
Exercise 4.1. In how many ways can $6$ students stand in a row for a photo?
Correction

All $6$ get a position: $6! = \boxed{720}$.

Exercise 4.2. How many ways to order $7$ different books on a shelf?
Correction

$7! = \boxed{5{,}040}$.

Exercise 4.3. How many possible final rankings (no ties) in a race of $8$ athletes?
Correction

A ranking is an arrangement of all $8$: $8! = \boxed{40{,}320}$.

5. Ranked $k$ of $n$ → $P(n,k)$

$P(n,k) = n(n-1)\cdots(n-k+1) = \dfrac{n!}{(n-k)!}$.
Exercise 5.1. How many possible podiums (gold, silver, bronze) in a race of $12$ runners?
Correction

$3$ distinct "seats", order matters, no repetition:

$P(12,3) = 12 \cdot 11 \cdot 10 = \boxed{1{,}320}$.

Exercise 5.2. A club of $15$ members elects a president and a vice-president (different people). How many outcomes?
Correction

$P(15,2) = 15 \cdot 14 = \boxed{210}$.

Exercise 5.3. How many $4$-letter codes can be formed from $10$ distinct letters if no letter repeats?
Correction

$P(10,4) = 10 \cdot 9 \cdot 8 \cdot 7 = \boxed{5{,}040}$.

6. Arrangements with repeated items → divide by repeats

$\dfrac{n!}{n_1!\,n_2!\cdots n_k!}$ — divide $n!$ by the factorial of each group of clones.
Exercise 6.1. How many distinct anagrams of "SUCCESS"?
Correction

$7$ letters: $3$ S, $2$ C, $1$ U, $1$ E.

$\dfrac{7!}{3!\,2!} = \dfrac{5040}{12} = \boxed{420}$.

Exercise 6.2. How many distinct anagrams of "ABRACADABRA"?
Correction

$11$ letters: $5$ A, $2$ B, $2$ R, $1$ C, $1$ D.

$\dfrac{11!}{5!\,2!\,2!} = \boxed{83{,}160}$.

Exercise 6.3. A signal is a row of $7$ flags: $3$ identical red, $2$ identical blue, $2$ identical green. How many distinct signals?
Correction

$\dfrac{7!}{3!\,2!\,2!} = \dfrac{5040}{24} = \boxed{210}$.

7. Circular arrangements → $(n-1)!$

Round table: $(n-1)!$. Necklace/keyring (flips equal too): $(n-1)!/2$.
Exercise 7.1. In how many ways can $7$ people sit around a round table?
Correction

Fix one person as anchor; the others choose seats: $(7-1)! = 6! = \boxed{720}$.

Exercise 7.2. How many distinct bracelets from $5$ different charms (rotations and reflections identical)?
Correction

$\dfrac{(5-1)!}{2} = \dfrac{24}{2} = \boxed{12}$.

Exercise 7.3. $8$ people sit around a round table, but Amal and Badr insist on sitting together. How many seatings?
Correction

Glue Amal+Badr into one block → $7$ objects around a circle: $(7-1)! = 720$.

The block has $2$ internal orders: $720 \times 2 = \boxed{1{,}440}$.

8. Subsets → $\binom{n}{k}$

$\dbinom{n}{k} = \dfrac{n!}{k!\,(n-k)!}$ — unordered selection.
Exercise 8.1. Choose a committee of $4$ from $15$ students (no roles). How many committees?
Correction

Group, no order: $\dbinom{15}{4} = \dfrac{15 \cdot 14 \cdot 13 \cdot 12}{4!} = \boxed{1{,}365}$.

Exercise 8.2. How many $5$-card hands from a standard $52$-card deck?
Correction

$\dbinom{52}{5} = \boxed{2{,}598{,}960}$.

Exercise 8.3. A pizzeria offers $10$ toppings. How many pizzas with exactly $2$ different toppings?
Correction

$\dbinom{10}{2} = \dfrac{10 \cdot 9}{2} = \boxed{45}$.

9. Repetition, order ignored → stars & bars

$k$ identical items into $n$ types/boxes: $\dbinom{n+k-1}{k}$.
Exercise 9.1. Distribute $12$ identical balloons among $3$ children. How many ways?
Correction

$n = 3$ boxes, $k = 12$ stars: $\dbinom{3+12-1}{12} = \dbinom{14}{12} = \dbinom{14}{2} = \boxed{91}$.

Exercise 9.2. How many solutions of $x + y + z + w = 9$ in non-negative integers?
Correction

$n = 4$ variables, $k = 9$: $\dbinom{4+9-1}{9} = \dbinom{12}{9} = \dbinom{12}{3} = \boxed{220}$.

Exercise 9.3. A café sells $4$ types of drinks. In how many ways can a group order $8$ drinks (types may repeat, only quantities matter)?
Correction

$\dbinom{4+8-1}{8} = \dbinom{11}{8} = \dbinom{11}{3} = \boxed{165}$.

10. "Exactly … of each kind" → choose per group, multiply

Quotas: $\dbinom{\cdot}{a}\dbinom{\cdot}{b}\cdots$ — then multiply. "At least" → sum the exact cases.
Exercise 10.1. Form a committee of $2$ professors (from $5$) and $3$ students (from $9$). How many committees?
Correction

$\dbinom{5}{2}\dbinom{9}{3} = 10 \cdot 84 = \boxed{840}$.

Exercise 10.2. How many $5$-card hands contain exactly $3$ hearts?
Correction

$3$ hearts from $13$, then $2$ non-hearts from $39$:

$\dbinom{13}{3}\dbinom{39}{2} = 286 \cdot 741 = \boxed{211{,}926}$.

Exercise 10.3. A team of $6$ is chosen from $7$ women and $5$ men, with at least $4$ women. How many teams?
Correction

Split "at least 4" into exact cases $4, 5, 6$ women:

$\dbinom{7}{4}\dbinom{5}{2} + \dbinom{7}{5}\dbinom{5}{1} + \dbinom{7}{6}\dbinom{5}{0} = 35 \cdot 10 + 21 \cdot 5 + 7 = 350 + 105 + 7 = \boxed{462}$.

11. Binomial theorem — coefficient extraction

Term of $(a+b)^n$: $\dbinom{n}{k} a^{n-k} b^{k}$ — keep the constants' powers!
Exercise 11.1. Find the coefficient of $x^2 y^6$ in $(x+y)^8$.
Correction

Take $k = 6$ (power of $y$): $\dbinom{8}{6} = \dbinom{8}{2} = \boxed{28}$.

Exercise 11.2. Find the coefficient of $x^3$ in $(x+3)^6$.
Correction

Term: $\dbinom{6}{3} x^3 \cdot 3^{3} = 20 \cdot 27 \, x^3$ → coefficient $\boxed{540}$.

Exercise 11.3. Find the coefficient of $x^4$ in $(2x-1)^7$.
Correction

Term with $x^4$: $\dbinom{7}{4}(2x)^4(-1)^{3} = 35 \cdot 16 \cdot (-1)\,x^4$ → coefficient $\boxed{-560}$.

Trap: don't forget $2^4$ from $(2x)^4$ and the sign from $(-1)^3$.

12. Symmetry — $\binom{n}{k} = \binom{n}{n-k}$

Flip a large $k$ to a small one before computing.
Exercise 12.1. Compute $\dbinom{60}{58}$ by hand.
Correction

$\dbinom{60}{58} = \dbinom{60}{2} = \dfrac{60 \cdot 59}{2} = \boxed{1{,}770}$.

Exercise 12.2. Compute $\dbinom{25}{22}$ by hand.
Correction

$\dbinom{25}{22} = \dbinom{25}{3} = \dfrac{25 \cdot 24 \cdot 23}{6} = \boxed{2{,}300}$.

Exercise 12.3. Find all $n$ such that $\dbinom{n}{2} = \dbinom{n}{5}$.
Correction

By symmetry, $\dbinom{n}{2} = \dbinom{n}{n-2}$. Equality with $\dbinom{n}{5}$ forces $n - 2 = 5$, i.e. $\boxed{n = 7}$.

Check: $\dbinom{7}{2} = \dbinom{7}{5} = 21$. ✓

13. Pascal — with / without a special element

$\dbinom{n}{k} = \dbinom{n-1}{k-1} + \dbinom{n-1}{k}$.
Exercise 13.1. Verify Pascal's identity for $n = 15$, $k = 6$.
Correction

$\dbinom{14}{5} + \dbinom{14}{6} = 2{,}002 + 3{,}003 = 5{,}005 = \dbinom{15}{6}$. ✓

Exercise 13.2. Teams of $4$ are chosen from $10$ players, one of whom is the star. How many teams include the star? Exclude her? Check the total.
Correction

Include: pick the other $3$ from $9$: $\dbinom{9}{3} = 84$.

Exclude: pick all $4$ from $9$: $\dbinom{9}{4} = 126$.

Total: $84 + 126 = 210 = \dbinom{10}{4}$ — exactly Pascal. ✓

Exercise 13.3. Row $5$ of Pascal's triangle is $1, 5, 10, 10, 5, 1$. Build row $6$.
Correction

Each entry = sum of the two above: $1,\;1{+}5,\;5{+}10,\;10{+}10,\;10{+}5,\;5{+}1,\;1$

$\Rightarrow \boxed{1, 6, 15, 20, 15, 6, 1}$.

14. Absorption — $k\binom{n}{k} = n\binom{n-1}{k-1}$

Kill a stray $k$; or compute via $\dbinom{n}{k} = \frac{n}{k}\dbinom{n-1}{k-1}$.
Exercise 14.1. Verify the absorption identity for $n = 8$, $k = 3$.
Correction

$3\dbinom{8}{3} = 3 \cdot 56 = 168$ and $8\dbinom{7}{2} = 8 \cdot 21 = 168$. ✓

Exercise 14.2. Knowing $\dbinom{11}{3} = 165$, compute $\dbinom{12}{4}$ with one multiplication.
Correction

$\dbinom{12}{4} = \dfrac{12}{4}\dbinom{11}{3} = 3 \cdot 165 = \boxed{495}$.

Exercise 14.3. From $12$ people, count the (committee of $5$, chair) pairs in two ways.
Correction

Committee first: $\dbinom{12}{5} \cdot 5 = 792 \cdot 5 = 3{,}960$.

Chair first: $12 \cdot \dbinom{11}{4} = 12 \cdot 330 = 3{,}960$. Same count — that is absorption. ✓

15. Plug-in trick — $\sum_k a^k \binom{n}{k} = (1+a)^n$

$a{=}1$: $2^n$  |  $a{=}-1$: $0$  |  $a{=}2$: $3^n$ …
Exercise 15.1. How many subsets (of any size) does a set of $10$ elements have?
Correction

$\sum_{k=0}^{10}\dbinom{10}{k} = 2^{10} = \boxed{1{,}024}$.

Exercise 15.2. How many subsets of a $7$-element set have even size?
Correction

Alternating sum $= 0$ → even-size and odd-size subsets are equal in number, each $2^{7-1} = 2^6 = \boxed{64}$.

Exercise 15.3. Compute $\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{6} 4^k \binom{6}{k}$ without summing term by term.
Correction

Plug $a = 4$: $(1+4)^6 = 5^6 = \boxed{15{,}625}$.

16. $k$-weighted sums

$\sum_k k\binom{n}{k} = n\,2^{n-1}$  |  $\sum_k k^2\binom{n}{k} = n(n+1)\,2^{n-2}$.
Exercise 16.1. Compute $\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{6} k\binom{6}{k}$.
Correction

$n\,2^{n-1} = 6 \cdot 2^5 = \boxed{192}$.

Exercise 16.2. Compute $\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{4} k^2\binom{4}{k}$ and check by hand.
Correction

Formula: $4 \cdot 5 \cdot 2^{2} = \boxed{80}$.

Hand check: $1{\cdot}4 + 4{\cdot}6 + 9{\cdot}4 + 16{\cdot}1 = 4 + 24 + 36 + 16 = 80$. ✓

Exercise 16.3. Over all committees (any size) formed from $8$ people, what is the average committee size?
Correction

Total of sizes: $\sum_k k\binom{8}{k} = 8 \cdot 2^7$. Number of committees: $2^8$.

Average: $\dfrac{8 \cdot 2^7}{2^8} = \dfrac{8}{2} = \boxed{4}$.

17. Vandermonde — $\sum_k \binom{m}{k}\binom{n}{r-k} = \binom{m+n}{r}$

Two pools merge; special case $\sum_k \binom{n}{k}^2 = \binom{2n}{n}$.
Exercise 17.1. Compute $\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{5} \binom{10}{k}\binom{8}{5-k}$.
Correction

Vandermonde with $m = 10$, $n = 8$, $r = 5$: $\dbinom{18}{5} = \boxed{8{,}568}$.

Exercise 17.2. Compute $\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{6} \binom{6}{k}^2$.
Correction

Sum of squares: $\dbinom{12}{6} = \boxed{924}$.

Exercise 17.3. A bookshop has $9$ novels and $7$ comics. (a) How many ways to buy $4$ books? (b) With exactly $2$ novels?
Correction

(a) One merged pool: $\dbinom{16}{4} = \boxed{1{,}820}$ — this is $\sum_k \binom{9}{k}\binom{7}{4-k}$ collapsed by Vandermonde.

(b) The single term $k = 2$: $\dbinom{9}{2}\dbinom{7}{2} = 36 \cdot 21 = \boxed{756}$.

18. Hockey-stick — $\sum_{i=r}^{n}\binom{i}{r} = \binom{n+1}{r+1}$

Constant lower index, growing upper index → bump both indices of the result.
Exercise 18.1. Compute $\dbinom{3}{3}+\dbinom{4}{3}+\dbinom{5}{3}+\dbinom{6}{3}+\dbinom{7}{3}+\dbinom{8}{3}$.
Correction

Hockey-stick with $r = 3$, $n = 8$: $\dbinom{9}{4} = \boxed{126}$.

Exercise 18.2. Compute $1 + 2 + 3 + \cdots + 50$ with a binomial.
Correction

$\sum_{i=1}^{50} i = \sum_{i=1}^{50}\dbinom{i}{1} = \dbinom{51}{2} = \dfrac{51 \cdot 50}{2} = \boxed{1{,}275}$.

Exercise 18.3. The triangular numbers are $T_i = \binom{i+1}{2}$: $1, 3, 6, 10, \dots$ Compute $T_1 + T_2 + \cdots + T_6$.
Correction

$\sum_{i=2}^{7}\dbinom{i}{2} = \dbinom{8}{3} = \boxed{56}$.

Check: $1+3+6+10+15+21 = 56$. ✓
